# Biomimicry translation map

The collection treats nature as a model for behavior and structure, not as a decorative theme. Each biological observation is translated into an email decision the reader can feel.

| # | Campaign | Biological strategy | Translation into the email |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Welcome | Pioneer plants and protective canopies help new growth establish in stages. | The welcome unfolds as ROOT → BRANCH → CANOPY: personalize, explore, then contribute. |
| 02 | Onboarding | Ant colonies reinforce rewarding routes with signals and mark unrewarding routes. | One visible next action at a time; completion strengthens the route and reveals the next marker. |
| 03 | Newsletter | Mycorrhizal fungi link many nodes and move resources and signals through a distributed network. | One hub story branches into smaller nodes, ending with a “pass the signal” sharing action. |
| 04 | Seasonal | Migrating organisms respond to environmental cues and move when conditions change. | The campaign notices a cue, prepares the reader in layers, and makes the timely move the CTA. |
| 05 | Product launch | A kingfisher’s long, gradually widening beak enters water with little disturbance. | A fine-point hero opens on one promise, context reveals gradually, and the flow ends in a quiet handoff. |
| 06 | Event invitation | Some fireflies coordinate flashes through local visual signals. | The schedule is presented as three timed flashes that synchronize the audience around a shared moment. |
| 07 | Promotion | Flowers make a reward findable through visible and chemical signals. | High-contrast color points to one offer, one code, and one destination without competing messages. |
| 08 | Re-engagement | Dormancy pauses growth until conditions support an efficient restart. | The message removes pressure, preserves prior work, and offers both “resume” and “pause” paths. |
| 09 | Transactional | Hexagonal honeycomb cells enclose useful area with short shared walls. | Order, shipping, and total are packed into compact, repeated modules with a single tracking action. |
| 10 | Feedback survey | Starlings coordinate from the movement of nearby neighbors rather than a central controller. | One lightweight response becomes a local signal; three responses together shape product direction. |
